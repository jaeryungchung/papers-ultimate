## Summary
WrightHere is a generative AI-infused creative writing system that generates interactive 3D environments from children's written stories, allowing users to explore scenes, interact with characters through dialogue, and gather inspiration to overcome writer's block. The system supports the cyclical nature of creative writing through two modes—Writer Mode for composition and Dive-in Mode for exploration—enabling seamless transitions between imagination and story creation.

## Core Contributions
- A novel dual-mode writing system integrating AI-generated 3D environments (Writer Mode + Dive-in Mode) to support creative writing's cyclical planning-translating process
- Story Materials system that captures dialogues, memos, and characters from 3D exploration for later use in writing via drag-and-drop co-writing
- Real-time generation pipeline combining 360° environment generation, 3D character mesh creation, text-to-speech dialogue, and context-aware background music selection
- Demonstration of how spatial exploration and character interaction can catalyze narrative development and emotional engagement in children

## Methodology
- **System built:** Unity-based application using ChatGPT-4o as central controller, Skybox AI for 360° environments, Meshy API for 3D characters, NAVER HyperCLOVA X for TTS, and Musicfy-generated music library (9 tracks)
- **Study design:** Qualitative user study with one-hour story creation sessions followed by exploratory interviews
- **Participants:** 6 participants (4 elementary school students ages 9-12, 1 middle school student age 14, 1 high school student age 18)
- **Metrics:** Qualitative feedback through open-ended questions about positive/negative experiences; behavioral observations during use

## Key Findings
- Scene exploration and generation were the most frequently used features; participants developed narratives through spatial exploration and iterative scene refinement
- Character interactions drove both emotional engagement (P1 created companion for lonely character) and story progression (P2 asked sword "Can I fly with you?")
- Younger participants showed more unconstrained imagination with AI, treating characters as autonomous beings; older participants had more specific output expectations
- Story Materials effectively bridged ideation and writing—participants used memos to overcome writer's block while maintaining creative agency
- Processing speed directly impacted feature usage (faster scene generation used more than slower character creation)

## Limitations & Future Work
- **Limitations acknowledged:** Qualitative-only findings; PC-based interface limits full spatial interaction; Korean language users only; short one-hour sessions; VR version developed but unusable due to child hardware constraints
- **Future directions:** Quantitative measures (writing performance, confidence, ideation capacity); optimized generation speeds; more diverse audio options and customizable character aesthetics; cross-cultural/linguistic studies; longer-term engagement; future VR implementation when child-friendly hardware improves
